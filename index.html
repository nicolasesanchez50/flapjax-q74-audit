What the groups do and do not imply. Under the brief's rule — recipients sharing a first BNB funder are one operator — the funding data yields 6 groups, but only three of them carry identity evidence: the treasury itself (3 recipients it gas-funded), one 5-year-old external wallet 0x67f5d43c…, and one 2-day-old external wallet 0xcc4ca271…. Two further clusters are funded by infrastructure (a bridge relayer and an ERC-4337 paymaster), which says nothing about who is behind them. The remaining 10 recipients have never received BNB and therefore cannot be separated by this method at all. So: at most 15 distinct operators if every ungrouped address is treated as its own operator, and no lower bound better than 6 from funding evidence alone. Confirming “no shared funder” for those ten would overstate the evidence — the honest reading is that ten payouts are to receive-only addresses whose operator cannot be determined from the chain.

The same pair address had sent 11,644,873.28965106 FLAPJAX to the treasury earlier that day (block 129946225, tx 0xa52c8d9f…). Net effect: treasury → smart account → pool, so this recipient's balance is zero and its payout left the operator set within the day.
  • The 00:57 batch of five identical 10,000 FLAPJAX transfers to five fresh addresses (0x3d98800c…, 0x8c062dc5…, 0xca586965…, 0xdf4ff9d2…, 0xfd08fc47…) fired 14 seconds apart, followed by one 20,000 top-up to 0x8c062dc5.   • 0xd1cada31… was funded by a bridge relayer (0x2bb27b73…, labelled "Relay: Solver", nonce 223,609), not by an operator wallet. That funder identifies infrastructure, not a person, so this address is effectively ungrouped.

5. How to re-check everything

No explorer trust required. Any public BSC node reproduces the transfer list; the funder transactions are ordinary native transfers verifiable by hash. Example with a public endpoint:

curl -s https://bsc-dataseed.binance.org -H 'content-type: application/json' \
  -d '{"jsonrpc":"2.0","id":1,"method":"eth_getLogs","params":[{"address":"0x90c8889f428F9Ebb77BB8f15CAD3a50a9aC680df",
       "fromBlock":"0x7bd2b01","toBlock":"0x7c01879",
       "topics":["0xddf252ad1be2c89b69c2b068fc378daa952ba7f163c4a11628f55a4df523b3ef",
                 "0x0000000000000000000000007c34e9e21ee28a49ff0b84b61774119e6633359f"]}]}'

Note that public dataseed nodes cap eth_getLogs ranges (this audit chunked the day into 50,000-block windows against a NodeReal public endpoint); the filter itself is exact, so a range-capped node only needs more calls, not a different method.
